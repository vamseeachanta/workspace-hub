## Findings
1. The verifier has no trustworthy way to prove the approval gate it claims to enforce. Plan §Pseudocode says `verify_candidate(wed_receipt, hf_manifest, website_registry, build_receipt)` will “require independently approved children,” but none of those four inputs is approval evidence and no live GitHub lookup, approval-marker input, reviewed-plan SHA, or signature/attestation source is defined. A caller-controlled JSON contract could therefore assert approval and bypass P1. This contradicts `docs/plans/2026-07-16-issue-3559-field-explorer-cross-repo-program.md` lines 103–111 and 152–159.

2. The cross-repository evidence interfaces are undefined and inconsistent. The parent expects a `build_receipt` and claims to join deployment, routes, scans, and production evidence at lines 110–125 and 152–159, but its Artifact Map/Files to Change contains no closed schemas for publisher receipts, website build receipts, deployment receipts, or smoke results. `docs/plans/2026-07-16-issue-74-website-pinned-field-well-drilldown.md` lines 143–165 does not create a build-receipt artifact at all. Consequently the proposed verifier cannot distinguish authentic child output from arbitrary structurally convenient JSON.

3. The state machine has no durable state. `accept_or_retain(candidate, last_good, production_smoke)` promises to record rejected candidates and retain a rollback target at plan lines 162–166, but the Artifact Map and Files to Change at lines 80–92 and 170–180 define no candidate record, accepted-release record, last-good pointer, state store, locking model, atomic update, or recovery procedure. The verifier therefore cannot enforce rollback history or prove that `last_good` remained unchanged.

4. The parent acceptance claims exceed its planned verification machinery. Lines 216–224 require real generated routes, pagination, mobile, keyboard, JavaScript-disabled, CSP, XSS, link, revision-disclosure, and production checks, while the only parent test artifact is `tests/workflow/test_field_explorer_promotion.py`. Tests at lines 199–203 are fixture/synthetic checks, not browser or deployment checks. No browser harness, production URL inventory, deployment API, command list, receipt format, or evidence collector is included. A synthetic fixture passing cannot establish the production claims.

5. The rollback exercise is operationally ambiguous and potentially production-mutating. Lifecycle P5–P6 at lines 112–114 moves directly from “production smoke” to an R1→R2 update and R2→R1 revert, without specifying staging versus production, who authorizes each external mutation, how deployment identity is captured, what constitutes R2, or how an unsuccessful rollback is recovered. The runbook is deferred to implementation, leaving a correctness-critical external-action decision unresolved at approval time.

6. The Resource Intelligence evidence violates the repository’s mandatory evidence contract. Plan lines 55–76 provide prose assertions and bare SHAs but no file-existence output, issue titles, line excerpts, exact reproduction commands, timestamp, or captured output. `docs/plans/_template-issue-plan.md` lines 73–118 requires those artifacts, and `.claude/skills/coordination/issue-planning-mode/SKILL.md` §Step 1.5 makes reproduction mandatory for runtime-behavior claims. The plan explicitly alleges datasets-server revision failure and destructive/caught website build behavior, so “Reproduction proofs: N/A” at line 74 is invalid even though this review independently reproduced the HF claim.

7. The review state is already stale. Plan lines 229–237 say Gemini is `PENDING` and “review not run,” but `scripts/review/results/2026-07-16-plan-3559-gemini.md` is non-empty and records `UNAVAILABLE`. The paths at plan line 11 are also overwrite-prone, non-revision-stamped names despite the layered-planning rule requiring revision-stamped, non-empty review artifacts.
