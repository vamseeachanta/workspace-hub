## Critical Findings

### C1: CSA wiki pages will be gitignored on creation

The `.gitignore` contains:

```
/knowledge/wikis/*
!/knowledge/wikis/engineering/
!/knowledge/wikis/cross-links.md
```

Only `engineering/` and `cross-links.md` are un-ignored. The plan proposes creating `csa-z276-1.md` and `csa-z276-18.md` under `knowledge/wikis/marine-engineering/wiki/standards/`, a path that is explicitly gitignored. Line 210 mentions that wiki content under `knowledge/wikis/` is gitignored by default and execution must force-add the intended wiki files only, but:
- `git add -f` on gitignored files is fragile; future git operations or CI that respect `.gitignore` may skip these files.
- The plan does not propose modifying `.gitignore` to add a `!/knowledge/wikis/marine-engineering/` exception, nor does it list `.gitignore` in Files to Change.
- This makes the deliverable non-durable and conflicts with the L3 durability requirement.

Required action: either add a `.gitignore` exception for marine-engineering wiki, relocate CSA pages to the engineering wiki, or justify force-add durability with a concrete mitigation.

### C2: Marine-engineering wiki conventions do not include `standards/`

`knowledge/wikis/marine-engineering/CLAUDE.md` defines a wiki structure with `entities/`, `concepts/`, `sources/`, `comparisons/`, and `visualizations/`, but no `standards/` category. The plan acknowledges this but defers resolution to implementation time. This is a known-in-advance convention gap, not a runtime discovery.

Required action: resolve the taxonomy question in the plan by either adding a marine-engineering CLAUDE.md amendment as an explicit deliverable or relocating the pages to a sanctioned directory.

### C3: Missing summary artifacts are a confirmed blocker, not a risk

The Risk section says implementation may discover that summary artifacts are missing. Repo state already proves they are missing:
- `data/document-index/summaries/` uses SHA-256 content-hash filenames rather than human-readable doc IDs.
- No summary files matching `OCIMF-TANDEM-MOORING`, `CSA-Z276.1-20`, or `CSA-Z276.18` were found.
- Ledger entries lack a populated `doc_key`; they only carry human-readable IDs.

Because acceptance criterion #2 requires prerequisite verification before any write, the current plan will stop at step 1 of execution.

Required action: either add a prerequisite-resolution phase to this plan, split the prerequisite work into a blocking issue, or explicitly relax the #2207 prerequisite with justification.

## High Findings

### H1: Domain classification mismatch for OCIMF Tandem Mooring

The standards-transfer-ledger classifies all three targets as `domain: marine`, yet the plan routes OCIMF Tandem Mooring to the engineering wiki while sending CSA standards to marine-engineering. The plan never documents the routing rule.

Required action: explicitly document the domain-to-wiki routing rule and why OCIMF is treated differently from CSA despite the shared domain classification.

### H2: `doc_key` vs ledger `id` conflation

The provenance-reuse contract defines `doc_key` as a SHA-256 content digest. The plan expects wiki pages to contain `doc_key`, `source_ref`, `domain`, and `promoted_from`, but:
- the ledger entries provide `id`, not `doc_key`
- no content hashes are populated for these three standards
- existing wiki conventions such as `ocimf-meg4.md` use `sources:` rather than the proposed new fields

Required action: specify exact provenance frontmatter fields, how they map from current ledger data, and provide a concrete example consistent with existing wiki conventions.
