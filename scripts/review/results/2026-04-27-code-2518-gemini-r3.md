## Verdict
MAJOR

## Findings
1. **Unsafe unconditional kill in trap:** The `cleanup_provider_jobs` function is bound to the `EXIT` trap, which runs on every script termination. It unconditionally loops through `${pids[@]}` and runs `kill "$pid"`. Because this trap executes *after* the script has already successfully `wait`ed for those subshells to complete naturally, it sends `SIGTERM` to PIDs that have already been reaped. In shared CI environments or busy workstations, this introduces a severe race condition where recycled PIDs belonging to unrelated system or user processes could be arbitrarily killed.
2. **Robust error handling:** The rest of the implementation successfully achieves the hardening objective. Provider timeout constraints, graceful promotion of structured stderr reviews (with `empty provider output` fail-safes), and non-interactive `GEMINI_CLI_TRUST_WORKSPACE` injection are correctly designed and strongly verified by the new shell test matrix.

## Required fixes
1. Clear the `pids` array (`pids=()`) immediately after the `wait` loop completes. This leaves the `INT` and `TERM` traps intact to clean up runaway providers if the orchestrator is aborted early, but prevents the normal `EXIT` trap from firing blind kill signals at already-reaped PIDs.

## Residual risk
1. **ARG_MAX limits on inline injection:** The `codex` and `gemini` invocations continue to inject the raw plan body into their argument vectors (`"$combined"`). While safe for current plan sizes, extraordinarily large plans could eventually breach environment `ARG_MAX` limits (typically ~2MB on modern Linux) leading to "Argument list too long" errors. This is a pre-existing limitation carried forward.
