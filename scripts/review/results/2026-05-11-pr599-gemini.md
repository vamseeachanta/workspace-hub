### Summary
The approach to formalizing the semantic equivalence contract and the registry pattern is solid. The reconciliation tests and scaffolded OQ closures demonstrate good rigor. However, the OQ-4 normalization at the compare site and the redundancy in the registry's pending entries present minor architectural concerns that should be addressed to ensure long-term maintainability and strict claim boundaries.

### Issues Found
- OQ-4 Fix Location: Handling Yes/true and No/false normalization at the compare site in `values_equal()` rather than at the dump site leaks serialization quirks into the comparison logic. While it preserves OrcaFlex load-side conventions, it prevents the YAML from being fully canonical. (MINOR)
- Registry Redundancy: Using both `pending: true` and `test_enforcing: null` creates redundant state in `MODEL_CLAIM_REGISTRY.yaml`. The registry should ideally only contain attested claims. (MINOR)
- OQ-1 Conditional Classification: Relying on free text for the spectrum-class subset (API/NPD/ESDU) introduces brittleness and risk of silent failures due to typos. (MINOR)

### Suggestions
- Refactor the spectrum-class subset in OQ-1 to use a strict Enum or code constant rather than free text to enforce validation.
- Consider splitting the registry into `MODEL_CLAIM_REGISTRY.yaml` for strictly attested claims and a separate `MODEL_CLAIM_INVENTORY.yaml` for pending items to guarantee the authoritative boundary.
- For OQ-4, consider standardizing booleans at the dump site to create a canonical representation, and handle OrcaFlex-specific translation at the load/export boundary instead of the compare site.
