## FINDINGS

1. **Complexity tier vs. actual blast radius (structural).** Plan §Complexity (line 719–721) labels this **T2** ("bounded to … rendering, timeout behavior, and cron-health interpretation"). The Files-to-Change table (lines 506–524) tells a different story: a **new shared module** (`cron_render.py`), three **backward-compat signature widenings** (`select_tasks`, `classify_line`, `plan_cutover`), a metadata-preserving classifier, catalog-owned dedup, plus edits to the **live crontab cutover path** (`cron_apply.py` `run_cutover`/`plan_cutover`). That is a systemic refactor of the cron control plane, i.e. T3 by the SHARED_SOUL scale (cross-provider/systemic). The mismatch matters because three genuinely independent, low-risk fixes (hygiene timeout `10→30`/`45→120`, cron-health marker parsing, `uv` PATH) are **bundled** with the high-risk renderer/selector refactor — so the safe fixes cannot ship until the risky one passes, and a rollback of the refactor drags them with it.

2. **The timeout fix's central evidence is warm-cache and the acceptance gate permits skipping cold-cache (evidentiary).** The only full-set timing is `elapsed=16.13` (plan lines 234–236), and Risk line 712 concedes it "may have benefited from filesystem cache." The acceptance criterion (line 590) accepts "an explicit cache-state disclosure **if the filesystem cannot be cold-started**." Combined, this makes the timeout fix's efficacy partly unfalsifiable: a reviewer can pass by disclosing "couldn't cold-start." Since the defect being fixed *is* a cold/slow `git status` timing out at 10s, the fix's proof should require at least one cold-or-dropped-cache measurement, not an opt-out.

3. **`TOTAL_TIMEOUT_SEC=480` leaves only a 120s margin and coverage is single-host (residual risk).** Gap is `45 5 − 35 5 = 600s`; default total stays `480` (verified `repo-ecosystem-hygiene-audit.sh:39`). Acceptance (line 590) explicitly disclaims cross-machine coverage ("cross-machine coverage is not implied by one host's run"). A machine carrying a larger `non_tier1_machine_access_current` repo set (the bucket the plan itself adds to enumeration, registry line 77) could exceed 480 on first cold run with no gate to catch it. Disclosed but unmitigated; acceptable only if you accept per-machine partial coverage (consistent with the empirical-coverage must-fire rule).

4. **Resource-intel framing overstates the selection bug's scope (minor accuracy).** Plan line 16 says "catalog tasks pinned only to `ace-linux-1` therefore miss the exact legacy match." Verified: the overwhelming majority of catalog tasks are pinned `[dev-primary, ace-linux-1]` (e.g. lines 18, 53, 69, 104…) and would **not** be missed once `dev-primary` is the resolved id. Only `solver-watch-results` and `solver-dashboard` are pinned `machines: [ace-linux-1]` *only* (lines 667, 684) with role `sim-worker` ∉ dev-primary's `[control-plane]`. The bug is real but affects 2 tasks, not the broad class the wording implies. (The repro at line 176 — `solver-*: False` — and the TDD coverage at lines 539/582 are correct; only the prose is loose.)

5. **`select_tasks` now selects on `role_match OR legacy_match`, admitting role-excluded tasks by machine pin (verify, not block).** The solver fix works precisely because machine-token match overrides role exclusion — dev-primary runs `sim-worker`-roled tasks because they carry its hostname token. That is intended parity with `setup-cron.sh` (machine-token-only), and both directions are tested (lines 539, 540). Flagging only so it is a conscious decision: the role model (`harness_profile.roles`) is now advisory for any task carrying a `machines:` pin, not authoritative. Confirm that is the desired contract for the whole catalog, not just the two solver tasks.

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- The plan's *correctness* is sound, but its *packaging* is the risk — bundling zero-coupling safe fixes with a live-crontab refactor is the kind of blast-radius decision that SHARED_SOUL's "enforcement scripts must not block their own artifacts" and "verify coverage empirically" rules are designed to catch at the seams.
- The most reliable adversarial signal here wasn't a false file path (all 9 cited locations verified) but an *evidentiary loophole*: a warm-cache benchmark plus an opt-out clause can make a timeout fix look proven without proving the failure mode it targets.
- A `role_match OR legacy_match` selector quietly demotes the role model to advisory for any machine-pinned task — a semantic shift worth ratifying catalog-wide, not just for the 2 tasks that motivated it.
