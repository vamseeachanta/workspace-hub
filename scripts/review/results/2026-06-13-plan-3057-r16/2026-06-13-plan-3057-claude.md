## Findings

1. **Internally contradictory timing evidence undermines the defect-#3 timeout sizing (the strongest finding).** The plan's two standalone probes (§Evidence lines 212–216) show workspace-hub `git status --porcelain=v1 --untracked-files=all` = **11.81s** and digitalmodel = **10.41s** — 22.2s for two repos. But the full **17-repo** audit (lines 235–242) reports **elapsed=16.13s** total. I verified the audit probes repos **sequentially** (`repo-ecosystem-hygiene-audit.sh:635/680`, no `ThreadPool`/`xargs -P`/`&`). 22.2s for two repos cannot fit inside a 16.13s sequential run of all seventeen. The only consistent explanation is that the standalone probes were cold-cache and the audit ran warm (the standalone `git status` calls themselves warmed the page cache for those two repos). That means the headline "16.13s" is a ~10×-optimistic warm number, and the plan's own data implies a realistic **cold** full-set time near 17 × ~10s ≈ 170s. The plan already defers cold/dropped-cache proof to closeout (acceptance line 603) and discloses a cache caveat (Risk line 732), so the design isn't broken — but the 16.13s figure must not be cited as worst-case reassurance, and the 22.2s-vs-16.13s discrepancy should be reconciled in §Evidence. This is the entire empirical basis for choosing `TOTAL_TIMEOUT_SEC=480`; right now it is unproven against cold cache.

2. **Acceptance line 603 is structurally satisfiable only because the margin is large, but the plan never states the expected cold full-set magnitude.** Given finding #1, the plan should assert the predicted cold-cache ceiling (e.g., "≤ N×120s repo budget, bounded structurally below 480s by per-repo deadlines even if every probe hits its budget") rather than leaning on the 16.13s observation. As written, a cold run that legitimately lands at ~150–200s would "pass," but a reviewer cannot tell from the plan whether 480s has real headroom or is one slow disk away from `incomplete_due_to_deadline=true`. Tightening the §Risk line 732 wording to give the structural worst-case (17 repos × probe budget) would make the sizing falsifiable instead of cache-dependent.

3. **Post-fix conflict count is not stated; the conflict-reporting path may become dead for the real catalog.** The reproduction shows `conflicts_count 5` (line 183). The catalog contains exactly the Windows tasks the plan filters out before conflict reporting (`provider-dream-bridge-win`, `hermes-claude-bridge-win`, `win-repository-sync`, `win-session-state-commit`, plus one more `scheduler: windows-task-scheduler` entry). If all 5 current conflicts are those Windows entries, the new `scheduler != cron` filter drives real-catalog `conflicts` to 0, leaving the `roles_authoritative` conflict-reporting branch exercised only by synthetic fixtures (`test_cron_apply_does_not_select_machine_excluded_role_matches`). Not a correctness defect — but the plan should confirm whether any of the 5 current conflicts is a genuine cron-scheduler conflict that must survive the filter, so a real regression isn't masked by "conflicts dropped as intended."

4. **Scope is T2 but the change exhibits T3 cross-module coupling (Claude r14 raised, consciously declined).** The plan introduces a four-module invariant: `cron_render.py`, `cron_transaction.py`, `cron_apply.py`, and `cron-audit.py` must all agree on raw+rendered catalog keys, the metadata-preserving classifier, and selected-task-id-aware catalog-owned dedup.
