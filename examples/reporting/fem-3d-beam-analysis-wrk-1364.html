Scope

Objective: Perform 3D finite element beam analysis of the GT1R R35 parachute mounting frame using CalculiX B32R Timoshenko beam elements with PIPE cross-section to determine stress distribution, deformation, load distribution across members, and reaction forces under parachute drag loading.

Inclusions

  • 3D beam FEM with 15 nodes, 16 B32R elements, and PIPE cross-section
  • Three load cases: 200 MPH single chute, 250 MPH single chute, 250 MPH dual chute
  • Von Mises stress distribution and utilisation ratios
  • Load distribution and strain across all frame members
  • Reaction forces at all boundary condition nodes (C3 fixed, B1 bolted)

Exclusions

  • Nonlinear material behavior (elastic analysis only)
  • Connection detail stress (bolt holes, weld toes — see WRK-1367)
  • Dynamic/impact loading (static equivalent applied)
  • Buckling analysis

Limitations

  • Linear elastic analysis — utilisation > 1.0 indicates yield exceedance, not actual failure
  • PIPE section assumes uniform wall thickness; local thinning at bends not modeled
  • B32R reduced integration may underestimate peak stress at sharp transitions

Validity Range: Elastic regime only; results valid for utilisation < 1.0

Design Basis

  • CalculiX v2.21 — Timoshenko beam theory (B32R element)
  • ASTM A519 — 4130 chromoly steel mechanical properties

Methodology

3D beam finite element analysis using two methods: 1. CalculiX v2.21 with B32R (3-node quadratic, reduced integration) beam elements and PIPE cross-section — used for stress/displacement results. 2. Independent 3D direct stiffness solver with 6-DOF Euler-Bernoulli beam elements — used for reaction force verification and load distribution. The CalculiX B32R+PIPE formulation was found to produce asymmetric transverse reaction forces (Fx, Fz) on this symmetric structure, with ΣFx equilibrium error of ~11% of applied load. This is a known reduced-integration artefact. Reaction forces are therefore taken from the independent solver, which achieves machine-precision equilibrium and exact symmetry. Frame geometry: 15 nodes, 16 members from WRK-1360. Boundary conditions: C3 nodes (N7, N10) fixed in all 6 DOFs; B1 nodes (N12, N14) fixed in all 6 DOFs. Drag force applied at coupler pin (N2) in -Y direction.

Standard: 3D Direct Stiffness Method (Euler-Bernoulli beam), verified against CalculiX v2.21

Combined stress criterion for ductile materials
(eq1)

$$UR = \frac{\sigma_{vm,max}}{F_y}$$
Ratio of maximum von Mises stress to yield strength; UR > 1.0 indicates yield exceedance

Validation

Method: Multi-method validation: (1) Independent 3D direct stiffness solver confirms exact equilibrium (ΣFx=0, ΣFy=F_applied, ΣFz=0 to machine precision) and perfect symmetry (all L/R force pairs equal). (2) WRK-1341 cantilever benchmark — B32R deflection vs PL³/3EI, 0.35% error. (3) CalculiX B32R+PIPE limitation documented — transverse reaction forces (Fx, Fz) unreliable due to reduced integration artefact (ΣFx error ~11% of applied load). Reaction forces in this report are taken from the independent solver; stress/displacement results from CalculiX.

Conclusions

Adequacy: INADEQUATE — The current tube section (1.75" OD × 0.120" wall, 4130 chromoly normalized) is significantly overstressed under all three drag loading cases. Utilisation ratios range from 3.07 (Case 1) to 6.66 (Case 3, governing). The frame exceeds yield capacity by 3–7× under parachute deployment loads.

Governing Check: Case 3 (250 MPH dual chute) — UR = 6.66 (max von Mises = 419,703 psi vs Fy = 63,000 psi). C3 frame rail mounts carry 87.1% of Fy load (22,977 lbs each, |F| = 24,013 lbs each). B1 bolted connections carry 12.9% of Fy load (3,388 lbs each, |F| = 9,776 lbs each). Load distribution is perfectly symmetric (verified by independent solver to machine precision).

Recommendations

  • Investigate larger tube section (e.g., 2.0" OD × 0.188" wall) or heat-treated 4130 (Fy = 100 ksi)
  • Check C3 weld capacity for 24,013 lbs reaction force per side (WRK-1367)
  • Check B1 bolt group for 9,776 lbs reaction per side — 6× M8 bolts (WRK-1367)
  • Consider stiffening at V-strut junction (high stress concentration area)
  • For production-grade analysis, use shell or solid FEM elements to avoid B32R+PIPE limitations

Assumptions
  1. All connections are rigid (welded joints modeled as continuous beam)
  2. B1 bolted connections are fully fixed (6-DOF constrained) — conservative
  3. Load applied as concentrated force at coupler pin node (N2) in -Y direction
  4. Material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linear elastic
  5. Cross-section is constant along each member (uniform PIPE)
  6. Self-weight neglected (small relative to drag forces)
