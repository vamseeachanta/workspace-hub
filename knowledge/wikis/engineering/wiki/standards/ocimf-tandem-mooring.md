# OCIMF Tandem Mooring & Offloading Guidelines for Conventional Tankers at F(P)SO Facilities

First-edition 2009 OCIMF guidance covering tandem mooring and offloading operations between conventional tankers and floating production, storage, and offloading (FPSO) or floating storage and offloading (FSO) facilities. Issued by the Oil Companies International Marine Forum (29 Queen Anne's Gate, London SW1H 9BU; ISBN 978 1 905331 62 8; eBook ISBN 978 1 905331 92 5). The guideline addresses the full operational envelope from offloading philosophy through subsea mooring arrangements, basis of design, the tandem mooring configuration itself, and the equipment used to make and break the connection between the export tanker and the F(P)SO.

## Scope (curated from source summary)

The OCR preview confirms the following table-of-contents scope for the 1st-Edition 2009 guideline:

- **Scope and purpose** — operational guidance for conventional tankers calling on F(P)SO facilities for tandem-mooring offloads (rather than side-by-side or single point mooring (SPM) configurations).
- **Applicable codes and standards** — establishes the regulatory and industry-standard envelope into which the guideline fits, complementing OCIMF Mooring Equipment Guidelines (MEG) for steady-state moored operations at SPM/CALM/SALM and quay berths.
- **FPSO/FSO offloading philosophy** — articulates the operational philosophy for export-tanker offloading from a weather-vaning floating production unit, including approach, connection, cargo transfer, and disconnection phases.
- **Subsea mooring arrangements** — covers the F(P)SO's own permanent mooring system and the implications it has for tandem-tanker positioning during offloads.
- **Basis of design** — environmental, operational, and equipment design assumptions underpinning the tandem-mooring configuration.
- **Tandem mooring configuration** — the geometric and operational arrangement in which the export tanker rides at a stand-off distance directly astern (or ahead) of the F(P)SO, connected by a primary hawser system.

## Equipment (curated from source summary)

The guideline's equipment chapter covers the hawser-system components required for safe tandem-mooring connection and disconnection. The OCR-extracted table of contents enumerates:

- **Single and dual hawser systems** — primary load-bearing connection between the export tanker bow and the F(P)SO stern (or bow, depending on configuration). Single-hawser is the historic baseline; dual-hawser is used where redundancy or load sharing is required.
- **Chafe chain** — high-strength chain segment installed at the tanker-end fairlead to absorb the chafe and abrasion that would otherwise destroy a synthetic or wire hawser at the contact point.
- **Fairlead** — the deck fitting through which the hawser is led from the F(P)SO winch to the tanker bow; its geometry, lining, and load-rating determine the safe working envelope of the connection.
- **Weak link** — a deliberately load-rated component placed in the hawser system so that, in an over-tension event, failure occurs at a known location rather than at an unpredictable point along the hawser.
- **Hawser handling** — the operational procedures for paying out, recovering, and maintaining tension on the hawser during connection, cargo transfer, and disconnection.
- **Hawser storage and retirement** — shore-side and on-board storage practices to preserve hawser integrity between deployments, and end-of-life retirement criteria to prevent in-service failures.
