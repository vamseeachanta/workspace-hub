Engineering reporting conventions

Proposed consolidation · Revision P3 · 30 September 2026 · Tracking issue 3925

Status: review draft; adoption and ecosystem installation remain pending. This document consolidates existing conventions and recent owner feedback. It does not establish fleet installation, session acknowledgement, engineering acceptance or approval of renderer changes. Source coverage is bounded; inaccessible session history is not represented as reviewed.

The reporting standard will preserve engineering meaning while making a report readable without repository or session context. The latest explicit owner instructions will govern presentation where they conflict with older examples. Engineering basis, source rights, approval controls and archived evidence will retain their existing authorities. In this review draft, imperatives and declarative reporting conventions describe the proposed requirements for a report that explicitly selects this standard; should denotes advice. Future adoption statements do not establish installed requirements.

1 Scope and precedence

The conventions apply to formal engineering reports across CFD, structural analysis, fitness-for-service, riser analysis, marine dynamics and calculation assessments. Consistent layout and terminology across disciplines will reduce the effort needed to review an unfamiliar subject. Technical chat, email and working records retain the engineering register; dashboards, human-decision plan pages and review boards retain their functional layouts. The ten-section structure below governs formal reports, rather than forcing a full report into each message or operational board.

Conflicts will be resolved by explicit owner instruction and applicable date, within its stated scope. The latest owner-directed HTML reports and their saved review comments are the primary presentation references; older memories and guides are supporting evidence. A recent modification timestamp alone does not establish acceptance or supersession. The 27–30 September feedback will replace older F-labelled findings and shorter formal-report skeletons. Applicable contractual and client-approved requirements govern the deliverable unless an authorized change is recorded. Conflicts with this general presentation standard will be recorded and resolved before reformatting the affected content; no silently inferred exemption will remove necessary evidence or limitations.

2 Formal report structure

Use a contents list, a maximum of two numbered heading levels, and the sequence in Table 2.1. Every section and subsection opens with one or two sentences explaining its purpose and how its content relates to the assessment.

Opening sentences will name the engineering subject and the content directly. For example, “The loading conditions and corresponding hydrostatics are presented in Table 3.1” introduces the data more usefully than a generic statement about what the section intends to accomplish. Apply this impersonal, subject-specific tone consistently throughout the report.

SectionRequired content
1 Executive SummaryBrief project/component and analysis introduction, followed by concise headline bullets with supporting sub-bullets where needed. Include the governing result, criterion and disposition, decision-relevant limitations and recommended next action.
2 IntroductionPurpose, scope, assessed configuration, exclusions and report status.
3 Design DataSource data, geometry, materials, operating conditions, units, coordinate/sign conventions, assumptions and acceptance criteria.
4 Analysis MethodologyEngineering method, governing equations, modelling approach, boundary conditions, solver/version, verification method and applicable comparisons.
5 Detailed ResultsCurrent results, readable plots and tables, governing cases, physical interpretation, evidence and verification/validation status.
6 Key FindingsNumbered subsections such as 6.1 and 6.2, each binding its conclusion to a result, comparator and governing case; no F-label findings.
7 LimitationsMissing or uncertain evidence, affected results, excluded phenomena, applicability limits and conditions required for acceptance.
8 RecommendationsEngineering actions, priorities, prerequisites and the result each action will resolve.
9 References9.1 Published references; 9.2 Internal references, using [I1], [I2] and subsequent reference markers where essential.
10 Acronyms and SymbolsDefinitions and units; distinguish reused symbols and dimensionless quantities.
Appendices A onwardSupporting tables, detailed mesh/model evidence, calculation detail, verification evidence, dedicated sensitivity investigations and relevant inconclusive checks.
Table 2.1 – Formal engineering report sequence.

Detailed findings and limitations will use regular-weight headline items with supporting bullets where lists are appropriate, limited to two levels. A section will not be filled with unsupported text merely to occupy a heading; inapplicability will be stated with its reason. The summary will retain limitations that materially affect its disposition, even when detailed evidence is in an appendix.

For benchmark summaries, name the assessed quantities and applicable criteria, governing deviations and comparison population. A pass percentage states its numerator, denominator and scope. Explain exclusion rules and counts; genuine zero-response evidence remains visible. Where relative error is undefined or unstable near zero, provide a justified absolute or scaled measure instead of silently counting or discarding cases. Conditional, unsampled and unassessed cases remain distinct.

3 Engineering register

The subject is the analysis, result, component, document or company. Methods use report-present passive, completed events use past tense, and findings use simple present. First-person plural remains a proposal-genre convention rather than assessment narration.

A limitation that affects a result is not defensive narration and will remain explicit. Body prose, findings and bullet labels use regular weight. Structural headings and table headers retain their semantic hierarchy; inline bold emphasis is omitted. State the current engineering facts without repetitive audit history, approval chronology, owner-direction attributions, hash-verification language or defensive narration. Preserve required revision control and evidence traceability in their designated records, and retain any technical change or limitation that affects interpretation.

  • Bind every conclusion to its criterion, comparator and governing case. Qualify measured values by the measurement date or applicable condition.
  • Use should and is recommended for advice; shall and must denote requirements.
  • State what is not established, identify the evidence gap and name the affected conclusion. Explain how acceptance depends on obtaining that evidence.
  • Use practical engineering language. Explain the physical method before its implementation. Remove promotional claims, unsupported certainty and operational narration that does not help the reviewer.
  • Define status terms quantitatively where the quantity permits it. If uncertainty cannot be bounded, identify the missing basis rather than inventing a numerical band. Terms such as wide, unbounded or not assessable require an explanation of their consequence.

4 Basis, methods and traceability

The report will expose enough of the basis for another engineer to check the result. Supplied, measured, assumed and derived values will be distinguished; an assumption ledger will state each assumption, its basis, sensitivity and affected result. Use “ASSUMED - to be confirmed: reason; confirm with evidence” where confirmation is required. Existing source-register IDs may remain in traceability columns; define their meaning and avoid opaque IDs as substitutes for physical descriptions.

Reader-facing engineering case identifiers, including defined geometry/configuration IDs and load-case designations, may be used when they are defined against physical conditions in a Design Data table before their first narrative use. They are distinct from opaque execution or audit identifiers. A schematic, table or case label will lead the reader back to its physical basis rather than require knowledge of the solver's internal run naming.

  • State geometry/configuration, load and operating cases, material basis, environmental inputs, units, sign/coordinate conventions and boundary conditions. Verify scope descriptions against every case in the claimed set, including appendages, exclusions and published result chains. Explain case/subset selection and separate scenarios that answer different engineering questions.
  • Use relevant CAD or engineering schematics to explain the physical model. Mesh/model detail will identify the represented components and convergence basis. Distinguish an omission from a schematic from an exclusion from the analysis. Drawings will reference Design Data; Design Data will reference qualified sources or labelled assumptions. Include physically consequential details such as adjacent diameters and added mass where applicable. Long supporting records belong in appendices.
  • Show governing equations, equation numbers and variable definitions with units. Provide a methodology flowchart where it clarifies the calculation sequence. Inspect source drawings and image-bearing PDF pages; text extraction alone does not establish that input tables or figures were read.
  • Mark quantities derived from supplied input and identify the source and transformation. Preserve source versions, stable dataset/run identifiers, hashes and computational lineage in the evidence records. Input trust, extraction verification, arithmetic reproducibility and analysis maturity remain separate dimensions; recomputation cannot qualify an unverified input.
  • In the formal report body, link section, figure, table, equation, appendix and reference citations to their actual internal targets. Printed labels remain meaningful. Citation markers link to bibliography entries; external source links are included only where verified and authorized for the audience. Keep query codes, run codes, decision/check labels, issue numbers, hashes and repository paths out of narrative prose. Essential technical locators belong in the report structure's Section 9.2 Internal references (Table 2.1), cited using [I1] style markers, subject to audience restrictions.
  • Within 9.1 and 9.2, distinguish model/data, solver/program and standards/method references. State software versions; state standard publisher, designation, edition/revision and clause. Standards-derived constants in calculation modules will retain the mandatory Citation sidecar and fail-closed contract. A missing detail will be marked pending instead of fabricated. Only verified applicable links will be included. Issue URLs remain hyperlinks within Section 9.2; this is the proposed formal-report placement of links, pending adoption, and does not amend the shared rule.

Criteria tables will include the engineering rationale, source or decision basis, units and normalization for each limit. Use benchmark criterion or target benchmark instead of unexplained tier labels. Proposed thresholds, damping assumptions and exploratory questions remain proposals until the applicable engineering decision is recorded.

Show labelled schematics covering the assessed configuration family and relevant dimensional and dimensionless proportions. Show the physical model, mesh and result together where that sequence explains the result; include governing result figures in the summary where they support its conclusions. Tooltips supplement a static table or note, rather than hide essential information. Plot analysis-domain coverage and define coordinate or heading conventions where relevant; contextual environmental data does not silently become project design data.

Report discretization relative to physical dimensions and relevant response length scales, alongside element sizes and counts. Describe alignment with important physical boundaries and the verified clipping, interpolation or integration treatment in each program. Material sensitivity claims require evidence. Distinguish native program output from responses reconstructed from its coefficients; agreement of reconstructed responses alone does not verify the native response solver.

5 Results, claims and uncertainty

A result and its evidential status will appear together. Verification, physical validation, analytical derivation, plausibility, pending work and engineering acceptance are separate claims; neither a renderer enum nor a coloured badge supplies missing evidence. State uncertainty for the quantity being compared: uncertainty on a difference is not automatically uncertainty on either absolute value. Explain correlation or cancellation assumptions. Do not fit or interpolate smoothly across physical regime or configuration changes without a justified basis; unresolved differences cannot establish a ranking.

Express each material limitation in one or two quantified sentences where possible: identify the assumption or sensitivity, its magnitude and what remains uncertain. If the magnitude cannot be bounded, state the missing basis instead of inventing a band. Do not shorten away necessary qualifications or turn an assumed sensitivity into a validated bound. Before comparing results, explain substantial differences in physical inputs, fluid properties, scale and normalization, together with any justified transformation. An unexplained discrepancy will remain an open engineering question rather than be concealed by formatting.

Detailed sensitivity investigations and material computational limitations belong in a dedicated appendix, cited from the affected result or numbered note. State whether auxiliary evidence was used only to interpret a result or to correct the reported value; show the basis of any applied correction, retain the uncorrected value in the correction record or sensitivity appendix, or a traceable reference to that evidence, and state the correction's uncertainty or why it is not established. The body and summary will retain the implications needed for the current decision.

  • Every result will identify its governing case, units, source and explicit evidence status. Confidence will not default silently. Display text will explain what an inherited status such as analytical or validated actually establishes.
  • Verification checks numerical implementation; physical validation requires appropriate measured evidence, directly or through a measurement-backed benchmark, with stated applicability and uncertainty. Analytical or manufactured-solution comparisons can support verification within the exercised regime. Cross-solver agreement alone cannot exclude common errors; archived-run agreement establishes reproducibility only. Conservation checks support verification of the particular invariant, not blanket correctness. Without a physical referent, report a verified prediction only where verification evidence exists. State the numerical uncertainty and that modelling error is outside that band when it is not bounded.
  • For this reporting standard, plausibility assessment states are implausible, not_implausible or not_evaluated. Here, not_evaluated records absence of an assessment, not a third assessed verdict or an implemented renderer enum. A plausibility result does not establish validation or acceptance. Named software-test and engineering-criterion outcomes remain distinct from plausibility verdicts.
  • Set tolerances before inspecting the assessed result and qualify check methods against cases where the expected behavior is established. If a band was selected after observing the values, retain the evidence plot, omit that retrospective band and mark the assessment not_evaluated. A later justified criterion requires a separately identified assessment. Include relevant comparator plots and comparable conditions; label unverified causal explanations as hypotheses.
  • Do not use colour as the only evidence-status cue. Preserve textual status, criterion and caveat on exported/static views as well as interactive views.
  • Remove superseded results from the current formal report, including greyed-out comparisons. Retain immutable source evidence and archived issued revisions. Name a material correction in the current revision and point to its correction record; update the summary, findings and recommendations consistently. A valid comparator is not a superseded result; identify its purpose and applicability.
  • Pending results may be listed with quantity, unit, status and expected evidence. A true zero, unknown, not applicable, not evaluated and pending value are different states; blanks and dashes require a legend. Placeholders do not satisfy issue or acceptance requirements.

Name each comparator class: closed-form, measured, cross-solver, conservation, archived-run or none, following the comparator rule. If archived-run is the only comparator, state that limitation in the report; reproducibility alone does not establish correctness. Apply the mechanism rule to causal claims: inspect the relevant source and output, or cite an applicable documented mechanism; otherwise retain a labelled hypothesis.

Present evidence before the concluding comparative observation in a results subsection. Quantify slower, larger or closer using a defined ratio, percentage or other measure. State the maximum deviation and governing case where informative, including the reference quantity and denominator. Each limitation identifies whether it is out of scope, a method/code restriction or missing data/evidence, with its practical consequence.

When computational performance supports a recommendation, compare equivalent cases and disclose model size, discretization, solution scope, hardware/resources, parallelism and timing definition. State unmatched conditions and verify comparable engineering outputs before drawing a speed ranking.

6 Tables, figures and numerical precision

Explain each figure or table before presenting it. Captions sit below the object and remain one short line; preceding text provides context and interpretation. Source details and qualifications belong in connected numbered notes below the object (or explicitly keyed within it), not in a long caption. Comparative plots will use consistent scales or explain why scales differ. Approved risk-matrix category meanings, colours, descriptions and legends will be preserved from their canonical source, rather than approximated visually.

  • Number figures and tables by section, using the form “Figure 5.1 – Caption” or “Table 3.1 – Caption”; appendices use “Figure A.1 – Caption”. Keep every cross-reference resolvable. Footnote markers in tables will link to numbered notes; notes will retain a readable printed form.
  • Put units in column headings and axes. Label legends, scales, reference frames and case names so a figure can be interpreted without internal codes. Align comparison conditions and state normalization.
  • Thickness and corrosion allowance use three decimal places under the house register. Other quantities use precision supported by the engineering purpose and input quality. Display digits never establish native-storage precision, solver accuracy or continuum accuracy.
  • Perform acceptance comparisons on qualified underlying values, not rounded display text. Disclose rounding-sensitive comparisons and retain native values and recovery/serialization metadata in the evidence.
  • Use actual evidence images. Screenshots supplement traceable numerical data and physical checks. Interactive views, including draft/configuration selection where relevant, will carry a labelled static/print fallback.
  • Client workbook or drawing precedents will be followed when applicable and source-verified, without copying project-specific assumptions into the general standard.

7 Human review and comments

Every reviewable engineering result will be presented for human review before acceptance or issue. A review pack will connect the evidence, physical expectation, assessment and reviewer decision. Stable finding/comment identifiers will remain in the machine-readable review metadata, with the report digest, quoted text and revision-specific section mapping; visible section numbers alone will not be persistence keys. This preserves traceability without restoring internal F-labels in the report body.

  • Each card will contain embedded evidence, comparator class, tolerance established before assessment, observed value, plausibility verdict, review focus and preparer recommendation.
  • Reviewer decisions and notes will be exported as JSON bound to the exact report revision and result identifiers. A generated recommendation, preselected option, absent reply or elapsed time is not an owner decision.
  • Supply a local commentable HTML copy with text/section selection, comments and Save/Load JSON. Prefer an explicit Save As destination beside the HTML where supported; otherwise disclose the Downloads fallback and provide Copy. A local-storage cache alone is not a durable review record.
  • When a page changes, identify its revision and require reload before further review. Preserve prior decided rounds and associate new comments with the revised artifact.
  • Disposition each comment as incorporated, deferred with reason, or requiring a decision. Verify saved JSON by reading it back; retain owner text privately where required.

Identify the intended human peer reviewer, or mark assignment pending. Preparatory self-review and generated recommendations remain distinct from independent review; acceptance stays pending until the authorized reviewer decision is recorded.

8 Document control and audience

Name the audience and surface before writing: internal working record, issued client deliverable, or hosted artifact. Apply source-specific rights and destination approval independently from the report's technical quality. Internal-reference entries will be rendered as audience-permitted document/model descriptions with revision and an accessible evidence route. Do not remove an entry while leaving a dangling [I1] citation; reconcile all citations in each exported edition. Client-owned engineering references need not be discarded merely because their source is internal.

  • Client deliverables carry the correct client/project/document identifiers and sufficient technical evidence. Internal cost models, machine names, per-run rates and internal tool-development narratives do not belong in client or hosted reports.
  • Material defects and limitations affecting a result will still be disclosed in engineering terms. Audience tailoring will never hide invalid results or remove a necessary limitation.
  • Hosted/share-link copies follow the established identifier-redaction policy even if nominally private. Client-owned details remain in the appropriate private issue channel. No private source excerpts or host identifiers will enter public standards or receipts.
  • Document control will record document identity, revision, date, purpose/status and responsible organization/discipline. Use the applicable approved revision scheme; internal draft rounds will not silently become issued revisions.
  • Render revision history when it exists; omit an empty first-issue history table. Archive issued revisions and preserve the change trail. Removing superseded results from the current report does not authorize deleting archives.
  • Retain client-supplied/measured originals and manifests under the owning private data contract. Vendor-licensed standards originals remain at their licensed locations; references do not grant copying rights.

Use a title describing the engineering assessment and a document number from the owning project or module register. Do not invent an ecosystem numbering scheme from one example. Connect proposed future work to the affected Key Findings and Recommendations using linked sub-bullets; keep proposed work distinct from completed evidence.

9 Production and issue checks

Use the existing report system for the target repository. The unresolved renderer-consolidation proposal does not authorize a third renderer or a migration. The presentation convention is independent of that implementation decision. The owner-selected HTML exemplar is the primary presentation reference, subject to the latest explicit comments and consolidated conventions; it does not itself establish offline operation, print quality, engineering acceptance or issue readiness. Check these properties independently for each delivered edition rather than copying incidental defects or treating a review-layer print rule as proof of report pagination.

  • Human-facing reports default to portable HTML; provide PDF or Word when required. Standalone HTML will embed necessary assets and avoid external CDN dependencies. Equations will remain legible without remote math rendering.
  • Escape untrusted strings and restrict link schemes. Do not add external scripts or analytics to a client review copy. Match generated report values, counts and revision links to the qualified source records.
  • Inspect actual rendered output: desktop, narrow screen, print and each supplied theme. Check clipping, captions, table continuation, notes, image visibility and link behavior. Verify interactive controls and a static fallback.
  • For PDF production, embed images and verify the delivered PDF using the applicable renderer checks. Where the delivery workflow uses Cairo and Poppler, compare their rendered outputs; differences require disposition rather than an assumed pass.
  • Read back every saved deliverable and record its digest. Distinguish local save, Git commit, remote publication, receipt and acknowledgement. Do not label a report issued solely because generation succeeded.
  • Before issue, confirm resolved review comments, current result set, evidence status, source rights, audience fit and authorized disposition. Failed or incomplete runs cannot contribute silently to result metrics or conclusions.

10 Adoption and source reconciliation
