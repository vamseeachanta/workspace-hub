Deckhand, inside Microsoft Teams

Your team drives real repository and project actions — from the chat workspace they already live in — with enterprise-grade authorization, scoped access, and a complete audit trail. No new app to learn, no console access to hand out.

The capability

Chat-driven operations, governed by design

Deckhand is an authenticated assistant that turns a Teams message into a controlled action on a defined set of repositories — then proves what it did.

💬

Where work happens

Operators ask in Teams; Deckhand executes against the right project. No context-switch to a terminal or a portal.

🎯

Scoped by project

Each conversation is bound to a scope — a named set of repositories with explicit permissions. A message can only touch its scope.

🧾

Provable

Every decision (allowed or denied) is recorded with the operator, the action, and the outcome — a durable audit trail.

Security posture — for your IT & security reviewers

Five enforcement layers, below the assistant

Guardrails are enforced beneath the model, not by prompting. The same floor applies on every platform.

LayerWhat it guarantees
Closed gatewayOnly explicitly allow-listed identities can talk to Deckhand at all. There is no "open to anyone" mode in your deployment.
Scope authorizationA message is authorized for exactly one project scope; it cannot reach repositories outside it.
Scoped access tokenEach scope runs under its own fine-grained credential. An out-of-scope repository is invisible — the request fails at the server (404), not just in our code. This is the real boundary.
No-destructive guardIrreversible operations (force-push, history rewrite, branch deletion) are denied. High-impact actions can require an in-chat approval card.
Audit + rate limitFull decision log; per-operator and per-scope throughput caps with duplicate suppression, fail-closed.

Teams transport adds Microsoft Bot Framework token validation on every inbound message, on top of the layers above.

⚠ One decision we need from you

How should Teams reach Deckhand?

Microsoft Teams delivers messages by calling a secure HTTPS endpoint (the bot's "messaging endpoint"). That endpoint must be reachable from Microsoft's cloud. How we expose it is a network & security choice that belongs to your team. Three options, ranked:

OptionHow it worksTrade-off
1. Managed tunnel recommendedAn outbound-only secure tunnel (e.g. Cloudflare Tunnel) publishes the endpoint over HTTPS. No inbound firewall change.Fastest, lowest exposure, stable hostname, free tier. Adds one managed dependency.
2. Reverse proxy on your edgeYou front the bot with your own proxy + DNS record + TLS certificate, opening a controlled inbound port.Maximum control, lives entirely on your infrastructure. More setup; opens a real inbound port to manage.
3. Host in your cloudDeploy the bot endpoint inside your Azure/cloud tenant behind your existing ingress.Cleanest tenancy story; highest lift; ties hosting to your cloud ops.

What we need: your IT/security team's preference among 1–3 (or a variant), plus confirmation that a tenant admin can complete the one-time Azure app registration for the bot.

Rollout

What standing it up looks like

1 · Confirm ingress (you)→ 2 · Azure app reg (tenant admin)→ 3 · Publish endpoint (us)→ 4 · Bind scopes + allow-list (us)→ 5 · Verify & go live

Your side (one-time)

  • Pick the ingress option (Slide 5).
  • Tenant admin: register the bot app + grant consent.
  • Name the internal users + Teams channels in scope.

Our side

  • Publish + secure the messaging endpoint.
  • Wire Teams channels to project scopes (closed allow-list).
  • Verify parity: destructive actions denied, out-of-scope repos invisible, audit confirmed.

The Teams assistant itself is already built; this is configuration + a one-time enterprise registration, not custom development.

The ask

With those, Teams joins your already-live Telegram and WhatsApp channels under one governed, audited control plane.
