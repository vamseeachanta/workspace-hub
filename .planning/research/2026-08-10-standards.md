# Research: engineering standards — 2026-08-10

## Key Findings

1. **DNV July 2026 Edition — Seismic guidance, floating wind integration, new class notations (FL(Y), FMS with fatigue qualifiers, NUI for unmanned).** DNV's annual standards refresh (released July 2026) includes updated seismic analysis methodologies (revised soil-structure interaction, foundation damping), new class notation FL(Y) for design fatigue life, updated FMS notation with selectable fatigue design factors, and NUI notation for normally unmanned installations. The edition emphasizes sustainability (carbon reduction), floating offshore wind (structural + mooring), and auditable engineering workflows. → [DNV Standards 2026: New Benchmark](https://www.inkl.com/news/dnv-standards-2026-the-new-benchmark-for-offshore-wind-and-green-energy-projects) | [DNV July 2026 Edition Press Release](https://www.dnv.com/news/2026/standards-now-available-the-july-2026-edition-of-the-dnv-class-rules-and-standards-for-ship-and-offshore/)

2. **API 579-1/ASME FFS-1 Part 16 — New section under development for Fiber Reinforced Polymer (FRP) equipment assessment.** The joint API/ASME fitness-for-service standard is expanding to cover FRP structures. Part 16 (Assessment of Fiber Reinforced Polymer Equipment) is under active development with technical updates targeted for 2026. This extends FFS methodology beyond traditional steel/pressure vessels to composite risers and structural components — a natural fit for subsea applications. → [API 579-1/ASME FFS-1 Update](https://utcomp.com/category/api-579-1-asme-ffs-1-update/) | [API 579 Part 16: FRP Assessment Levels](https://utcomp.com/api-579-1-asme-ffs-1-update/api-579-part-16-assessment-levels-for-frp-assets/)

3. **NORSOK M-501 Revision 7 (2026 Edition) — Enhanced surface preparation and protective coatings standard for offshore installations; cathodic protection design per NORSOK M-503.** NORSOK M-501 R7 (2026) refines protective coating system specifications (paints, metallic coatings, passive fire protection), emphasizing minimum-maintenance designs for subsea and splash-zone environments. Complements NORSOK M-503 cathodic protection requirements (sacrificial anodes, -800 to -1050 mV polarization, design current density, electrolyte resistivity models). → [NORSOK M-501 Systems Guide 2026 Edition](https://industrial.sherwin-williams.com/content/dam/pcg/sherwin-williams/protective-marine/emeai/gb/en-gb/pdfs/system-guides/NORSOK-M-501-Systems-Guide-SW-EN.pdf) | [NORSOK M-503 Cathodic Protection](https://www.scribd.com/document/126561224/Norsok-M-503-Cathodic-Protection)

4. **ISO 19902 Digital Integration — Real-time structural health monitoring tools now paired with ISO 19902 compliance tracking for design integrity and fatigue management (2026 industry adoption).** While the base ISO 19902:2020 standard is unchanged, 2026 industry practice shows integration of live environmental data (stress, corrosion, structural deformation) monitored continuously against ISO 19902's integrity thresholds. Implies calculation tools should emit ISO-19902-compatible fatigue/stress results suitable for SHM systems. → [ISO 19902: Essential Guide for Fixed Offshore Structures](https://community.trustcloud.ai/article/iso-19902-everything-you-need-to-know/) | [ISO 19902:2020 Standard](https://www.iso.org/standard/65688.html)

5. **Floating Offshore Wind Fatigue Benchmark — FLOATBench dataset (2026) released for FOWT tower fatigue analysis, validating multi-physics coupling (wind + wave + platform motion).** DNV's July 2026 seismic updates and the publication of FLOATBench (a structured fatigue-analysis dataset for floating turbine towers) indicate industry convergence on rigorous fatigue assessment for floating platforms. Directly relevant to Phase 999.2 (Wind Energy Vision) — fatigue analysis for floating foundations requires coupled aerodynamic/hydrodynamic/structural modeling. → [FLOATBench: Dataset and Benchmark for Floating Offshore Wind Turbine Tower Fatigue](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25717)

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`

**DNV's July 2026 edition is the watershed moment here.** The shift to FL(Y) notation (explicit design fatigue life, replacing implicit Palmgren-Miner assumptions) and FMS configurability signals that fatigue assessment is moving from one-size-fits-all formulas to scenario-based design factors. Your v1.0 digitalmodel fatigue module likely used fixed DNV guidance; v1.1 Phase 7's smoke tests should verify that client reports using the corrected 2026 edition. This isn't a cosmetic issue — a miscitation (citing DNV-2013 when you implemented DNV-2026) invalidates the design and fails certification.

**Floating wind is now the industry's consensus future, and your backlog reflects this opportunity unevenly.** FLOATBench's publication + DNV's July 2026 floating wind emphasis + all three competitors (Bentley, DNV, Wood) bundling floating fatigue + mooring coupling means **Phase 999.2 should not remain "backlog"** — it should be v1.2's primary execution lane. Floating platform hydrostatic + fatigue modules are defensible, high-ROI additions that position digitalmodel as the design-phase partner to cloud-based solvers (per August 6 competitor research). The economics have shifted: fixed-platform tools are commoditized; floating-wind expertise is scarce and expensive.

**NORSOK M-501 R7 and API 579-1 Part 16 are lower urgency but higher confidence.** Both are incremental expansions of mature standards (protective coatings, FRP assessment). Your existing CP and FFS modules (if present) don't need complete rewrites — they need spot checks against new guidance. M-501 R7 affects corrosion allowance and anode sizing; Part 16 opens a new market (composite risers) that doesn't exist in v1.0 but will exist by v1.2.

**ISO 19902's SHM pairing is the quiet infrastructure win.** No standard change, but industry practice shift: clients now pair OrcaWave/SACS/Sesam output directly to monitoring systems. Your v1.1 reports should emit machine-readable JSON with stress/fatigue results, not just PDFs. This is a v1.2 positioning advantage ("calculation to SHM integration") that costs little to implement now.
