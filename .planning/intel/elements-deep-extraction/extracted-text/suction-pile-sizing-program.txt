SUCTION PILE DEFLECTION CONSIDERATIONS AND ASSUMPTIONS

The structural deformation of a suction pile, much like a short beam, is principally due to lateral shear
since the overall length to diameter ratios are very low (3<L/D<7). Shear deformation is generally
negligible and can be omitted for general diameter and length sizing but can be used to determine a
practical cross sectional area requirement for the pile wall thickness.

Points of applied external forces acting on the pile introduce local shell deformation which is a detail
design activity for local stiffening and can be ignored for preliminary diameter and length sizing for cost
analysis.

With negligible bending and shear deformations, the global behavior of a properly sized suction pile is
dominated therefore by the rigid body motion of the pile in the deforming soil. The angular deflection
of a suction pile due to applied loads can be determined due to the soil system deformation as defined
by ‘p-y’ data in API RP-2A.

The vertical resistance and deformation can be determined by ‘t-z’ data in API RP-2A. The program uses
the t-z data to report the factor of safety against vertical pull-out relative to the estimated pile weight
and the specified vertical load. As in all soil analysis a factor of safety above 2 is considered acceptable.

Each of the soil layers is considered to act alone in its zone and is considered to behave in accordance
with the guidelines in API RP-2A.

SOLUTION METHODOLGY

The suction pile program resolves the rigid body rotation in a variety of layered soils that can be defined
by the API p-y and t-z data.

Lateral and vertical loads can be applied anywhere along the length of the pile but are defined at the
same location to represent an applied force vector.

Overburden pressure is determined for each vertical division of the pile length for determination of the
identified parameters utilized within API RP-2A including the ‘Xr’ value for clay soils. The ‘Xr’ value
which is the soil depth boundary between shallow and deep clay behavior is determined when the clay
strength affected by the cohesive shear strength, overburden pressure and pile to depth ratio is equal to
the deep clay ultimate strength ratio (=9*cohesive shear strength).
The program solution is determined with the accuracy of 1/100th of the applied load as an accuracy level
of the square of the sum of the squares of the force and moment equation residuals {[the difference in
the lateral applied force and the soil resistance force] and [the difference in the resulting moment of the
lateral applied force and the point of rotation to the resulting moment of the soil resistances and their
distances from the point of rotation]}. While the units appear mixed for the solution residuals, the
methodology ensures accuracy independent of the problem definition. This accuracy value is seen to be
adequate for practical applications. The upper allowable limit of iterations within the solution is 1,000
although a good pile estimate will likely converge in less than 50 iterations. The Newton non-linear
solution method is employed to resolve the unknowns.

Solution time for a well-defined and practical problem on a standard computer is less than 2 seconds.

PROGRAM INPUT AND OUTPUT

The input data format is shown in Appendix A following a successful execution with 10 layers of soil with
variations of soil type (sand or clay) and with variations of density and strength. The program maximum
limit is currently 10 layers of soil and considered practical since soil lab data is normally not available for
more closely spaced soil in the first 100 feet of soil depth.

The solution procedure divides the embedded section of the pile into individual segments to refine the
solution. There is no limit of the number of segments within the defined soil layers. The soil properties
defined at the center of a pile segment are used to determine the local deflection and the soil resistance
due to that deflection. The number of suction pile divisions is unlimited within the software however
divisions finer than one foot are not considered practical due to the accuracy of the layer depths and the
tolerances on the field and lab tests.

The pile data to be defined by the user includes:

    •   Pile Diameter;
    •   Wall thickness (optional for weight estimate used in the pull-out F/S calculation);
    •   Pile length;
    •   Pile Depth into the soil (after any scour considerations);
    •   Water table depth (for seabed piles=Pile depth; provided as a parameter for beach start-up pile
        estimates);
    •   Elevation of the applied lateral and vertical forces from the bottom of the pile;
    •   Horizontal force magnitude;
    •   Vertical force magnitude;
    •   Applied moment magnitude (normally zero);
    •   Plug indication flag (1 for plugged pile & 0 for unplugged pile; for sensitivity evaluations);
    •   Number of soil layers to be considered (if 20 layers are defined but 10 are considered, the last
        layer will be used for the positions below that layer).

Each layer of soil includes:

    •   Depth of the start of the soil properties below the mudline or soil surface;
    •   Soil type (sand [s] or clay [c]);
    •   Wet in-situ soil weight from lab or field report (dry air weight – buoyancy);
    •   Friction angle (if sand else ignored for clay);
    •   Cohesive shear strength (if clay else ignored for sand);
    •   Epsilon (the value of strain ratio [in/in] at 50% of the unconfined ultimate shear strength,
        without data use. 0.1).

The soil data is repeated for each layer. Layer numbers (not shown on the report printout but shown on
the input screen) are provided to assist in tabulating the soil layers. Property numbers (1-6) on the input
screen are provided to assist to prevent error.

The lateral resistance is computed with the non-linear relationships defined by API RP-2A (p-y data) for
both sand and clay soils at the number of equal divisions defined for the pile depth below the top of soil.

An assumption in the program is that the loads will be cyclic in accordance with API RP-2A providing
some degree of conservatism if there is a single static load condition.

The vertical resistance is computed with the non-linear relationships defined by API RP-2A (t-z data) for
both sand and clay soils.

The program includes the lateral shear at the pile bottom as a linear value of cross sectional area time
the shear strength without non-linear effects in addition to the rotational resistance of the p-y data.

The program solution is non-linear and resolved with Newton’s method. The convergence is relatively
quick for a valid solution. A graph is developed during the program execution indicating convergence.

In the event the problem posed does not converge, several conditions may exist. These are:

    •   The pile is too short for the applied loads and excessive rotation occurs;
    •   The pile diameter is too small for the applied loads and excessive rotation occurs;
   •   The soil has too little resistance for the diameter and length of the pile and excessive rotation
       occurs;
   •   The loads are so small for the diameter and length of pile that deflections are lost in numerical
       coarseness during the solution of the Jacobian matrix.
